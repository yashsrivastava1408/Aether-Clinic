TITLE: Asthma - Self-Management and Action Plans
SOURCE: Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA) Guidelines, 2024
CATEGORY: Pulmonology | Chronic Disease

Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by variable expiratory airflow limitation. It affects approximately 300 million people worldwide.

Symptom Recognition:
- Wheezing (high-pitched whistling sound during breathing)
- Shortness of breath (dyspnea)
- Chest tightness
- Cough (especially at night or early morning)
- Symptoms are variable and may be triggered by specific exposures

Common Triggers:
- Allergens: Dust mites, pet dander, mold, pollen
- Irritants: Smoke, strong odors, air pollution, cold air
- Respiratory infections (viral)
- Exercise (exercise-induced bronchoconstriction)
- Emotional stress
- Weather changes (cold, humid, or windy conditions)
- Medications: Aspirin, NSAIDs, beta-blockers in susceptible individuals

Asthma Action Plan (Traffic Light System):
GREEN ZONE (Doing Well):
- No cough, wheeze, chest tightness, shortness of breath
- Can do usual activities
- Peak flow: 80-100% of personal best
- Action: Continue maintenance medications

YELLOW ZONE (Getting Worse):
- Cough, wheeze, chest tightness, or shortness of breath
- Waking at night due to asthma
- Some limitation of activities
- Peak flow: 50-79% of personal best
- Action: Use quick-relief medication; contact healthcare provider

RED ZONE (Medical Alert):
- Severe shortness of breath, quick-relief medications not helping
- Cannot do usual activities
- Symptoms getting worse
- Peak flow: < 50% of personal best
- Action: Take quick-relief medication and GO TO HOSPITAL or CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES

Self-Care Best Practices:
- Keep rescue inhaler accessible at all times
- Use spacer with metered-dose inhalers for better drug delivery
- Rinse mouth after using corticosteroid inhalers (prevents oral thrush)
- Maintain trigger diary to identify personal triggers
- Regular peak flow monitoring if prescribed
- Annual influenza vaccination recommended
- Keep home humidity below 50% to reduce dust mite and mold growth

TITLE: Allergic Reactions and Anaphylaxis
SOURCE: World Allergy Organization (WAO) Anaphylaxis Guidelines, 2023
CATEGORY: Allergy & Immunology | Emergency

Mild Allergic Reactions:
- Localized urticaria (hives), itching
- Mild swelling at exposure site
- Sneezing, runny nose, watery eyes
- Self-care: Remove allergen exposure, cool compress, OTC antihistamines

Anaphylaxis - LIFE-THREATENING EMERGENCY:
Anaphylaxis is a severe, potentially fatal systemic allergic reaction with rapid onset (usually within minutes to hours of exposure).

Diagnostic Criteria (Any ONE of the following):
1. Skin/mucosal involvement (hives, flushing, tongue swelling) PLUS respiratory compromise (dyspnea, wheeze, stridor) OR cardiovascular compromise (hypotension, syncope)
2. Two or more of: skin involvement, respiratory compromise, reduced BP, persistent GI symptoms — after exposure to known allergen
3. Reduced blood pressure alone after exposure to KNOWN allergen

Common Triggers:
- Food: Peanuts, tree nuts, shellfish, fish, milk, eggs, wheat, soy
- Medications: Penicillin, NSAIDs, contrast media
- Insect stings: Bees, wasps, hornets, fire ants
- Latex
- Exercise (food-dependent exercise-induced anaphylaxis)

EMERGENCY ACTION:
1. Call emergency services IMMEDIATELY
2. Administer epinephrine auto-injector (e.g., EpiPen) into outer mid-thigh
3. Lay the person flat (if breathing difficulty, allow them to sit up)
4. Do NOT give oral medication if there is throat swelling
5. Second dose of epinephrine after 5-15 minutes if no improvement
6. CPR if breathing or heartbeat stops
7. After stabilization: ALL patients must be observed in hospital for 4-6 hours (biphasic reactions can occur)

TITLE: Skin Conditions - Primary Assessment Guide
SOURCE: British Association of Dermatologists (BAD) Patient Guidelines, 2023
CATEGORY: Dermatology | General Medicine

Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis):
- Chronic, relapsing inflammatory skin condition
- Appears as dry, itchy, red, rough patches
- Common locations: Creases of elbows, behind knees, face, hands
- Self-care: Moisturize frequently (emollients 2-3 times daily), lukewarm baths (not hot), avoid harsh soaps, identify and avoid triggers (wool, synthetic fabrics, certain detergents)
- Seek care if: Oozing, crusting, spreading rapidly, signs of infection (yellow crusting, increased redness/warmth)

Psoriasis:
- Chronic autoimmune condition causing rapid skin cell turnover
- Well-defined, raised red patches with silvery-white scales
- Common locations: Elbows, knees, scalp, lower back
- Triggers: Stress, infections (streptococcal), certain medications, cold weather, alcohol
- Self-care: Keep skin moisturized, gentle sun exposure (10-15 min), avoid picking/scratching scales, warm (not hot) baths with colloidal oatmeal

Contact Dermatitis:
- Irritant: Direct damage from chemicals (detergents, solvents, acids)
- Allergic: Immune reaction to allergens (nickel, latex, poison ivy, fragrances)
- Self-care: Identify and avoid causative agent, wash affected area thoroughly, cool compress, calamine lotion

ABCDE Rule for Moles (Melanoma Screening):
- A = Asymmetry: One half unlike the other
- B = Border: Irregular, scalloped, or poorly defined
- C = Color: Varied from one area to another (shades of brown, tan, black, sometimes white, red, or blue)
- D = Diameter: Larger than 6mm (pencil eraser size)
- E = Evolving: Size, shape, or color is changing

⚠️ If ANY of the ABCDE criteria are met, seek dermatological evaluation promptly. Early detection of melanoma is critical for survival.
