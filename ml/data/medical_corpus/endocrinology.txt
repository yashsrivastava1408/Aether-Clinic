TITLE: Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus - Comprehensive Management
SOURCE: American Diabetes Association (ADA) Standards of Care, 2024
CATEGORY: Endocrinology | Chronic Management

Diagnostic Criteria for Diabetes:
- Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L)
- 2-hour Plasma Glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during OGTT
- HbA1c ≥ 6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
- Random Plasma Glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL with classic symptoms

Prediabetes Range (Increased Risk):
- FPG: 100-125 mg/dL (5.6-6.9 mmol/L)
- 2-hour PG: 140-199 mg/dL (7.8-11.0 mmol/L)
- HbA1c: 5.7-6.4% (39-47 mmol/mol)

Target Glycemic Goals:
- HbA1c < 7.0% for most non-pregnant adults
- Pre-meal glucose: 80-130 mg/dL (4.4-7.2 mmol/L)
- Post-meal glucose (1-2 hours): < 180 mg/dL (10.0 mmol/L)
- Individualize targets based on age, comorbidities, hypoglycemia risk

Lifestyle Management:
- Medical Nutrition Therapy: Individualized meal plan focusing on carbohydrate consistency
- Physical Activity: 150+ minutes/week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity
- Weight Management: 5-7% weight loss for overweight/obese individuals
- Sleep: 7-8 hours/night; screen for sleep apnea
- Psychosocial support: Screen for diabetes distress and depression

Hypoglycemia Recognition (Blood Glucose < 70 mg/dL):
Level 1 (Alert): 54-70 mg/dL - Treat with 15-20g fast-acting carbohydrate
Level 2 (Clinically Significant): < 54 mg/dL - Requires immediate intervention
Level 3 (Severe): Altered mental status, requires assistance

Emergency Treatment of Hypoglycemia:
1. Consume 15g fast-acting glucose (glucose tablets, juice, regular soda)
2. Recheck blood glucose in 15 minutes
3. Repeat treatment if still < 70 mg/dL
4. Once normalized, eat a snack with protein and complex carbs
5. If unconscious: Do NOT give food/drink; call emergency services; administer glucagon if available

Diabetic Foot Care Protocol:
- Inspect feet daily for cuts, blisters, redness, swelling
- Wash feet with lukewarm water, dry thoroughly (especially between toes)
- Moisturize tops and bottoms (NOT between toes)
- Wear well-fitting shoes; never walk barefoot
- Trim toenails straight across
- Annual comprehensive foot examination by healthcare provider

TITLE: Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) - Emergency Recognition
SOURCE: International Society for Pediatric and Adolescent Diabetes (ISPAD), 2023
CATEGORY: Endocrinology | Emergency

DKA is a life-threatening complication of diabetes, more common in Type 1 but can occur in Type 2. It results from severe insulin deficiency leading to hyperglycemia, ketosis, and metabolic acidosis.

Warning Signs of DKA:
- Blood glucose > 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) with ketones
- Excessive thirst and frequent urination (polyuria)
- Nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain
- Fruity-smelling breath (acetone odor)
- Deep, rapid breathing (Kussmaul breathing)
- Confusion, drowsiness, or loss of consciousness
- Dehydration signs: dry mouth, sunken eyes, poor skin turgor

CRITICAL: DKA requires IMMEDIATE emergency medical care. Do NOT attempt to manage at home. Call emergency services immediately.

TITLE: Gestational Diabetes - Screening and Self-Care
SOURCE: WHO Recommendations on Gestational Diabetes, 2024
CATEGORY: Endocrinology | Obstetrics

Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) affects approximately 14% of pregnancies worldwide. It typically develops during the second or third trimester and increases risks for both mother and baby.

Risk Factors: Age > 25, BMI ≥ 25, family history of diabetes, previous GDM, polycystic ovary syndrome, certain ethnic groups (South Asian, Middle Eastern, African, Hispanic).

Self-Monitoring Protocol:
- Test fasting glucose every morning (target: < 95 mg/dL)
- Test 1-hour post-meal glucose (target: < 140 mg/dL)
- Test 2-hour post-meal glucose (target: < 120 mg/dL)
- Record all readings in a glucose diary
- Report readings above target to healthcare provider within 24 hours

Dietary Recommendations:
- Eat 3 small meals and 2-3 snacks daily
- Include protein with every meal and snack
- Choose complex carbohydrates over simple sugars
- Avoid sugary beverages and fruit juices
- Include fiber-rich foods (vegetables, legumes, whole grains)
