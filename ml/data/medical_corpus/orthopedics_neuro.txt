TITLE: Low Back Pain - Assessment and Self-Care
SOURCE: American College of Physicians (ACP) Clinical Guidelines, 2023
CATEGORY: Orthopedics | Musculoskeletal

Acute Low Back Pain is one of the most common presenting complaints in primary care, affecting up to 80% of adults at some point. Most cases (90%) are non-specific and resolve within 4-6 weeks with conservative management.

Red Flag Assessment (Rule Out Serious Pathology):
- Cauda equina syndrome: Bilateral leg weakness, saddle anesthesia, urinary retention/incontinence → SURGICAL EMERGENCY
- Spinal fracture: Trauma history, osteoporosis, prolonged steroid use, age > 70
- Cancer: Unexplained weight loss, history of cancer, pain at rest, age > 50 with new-onset
- Infection: Fever, IV drug use, recent surgery, immunosuppression

Non-Pharmacological First-Line Treatments (ACP Recommended):
- Stay active: Bed rest is NOT recommended and may worsen outcomes
- Superficial heat therapy (heating pad, warm bath)
- Massage therapy
- Spinal manipulation (chiropractic or osteopathic)
- Acupuncture for chronic low back pain
- Mindfulness-based stress reduction
- Progressive relaxation
- Yoga and Tai Chi for chronic low back pain

Exercise Recommendations:
- Core strengthening: Planks, bird-dogs, dead bugs
- Flexibility: Cat-cow stretches, knee-to-chest, hamstring stretches
- Walking: 20-30 minutes daily
- Swimming or water-based exercises
- Avoid: Heavy lifting, prolonged sitting, high-impact activities during acute phase

Posture and Ergonomics:
- Sit with feet flat on floor, knees at 90°
- Use lumbar support or rolled towel at the small of the back
- Monitor at eye level, arms at 90° for computer work
- Stand and stretch every 30-45 minutes
- Sleep on side with pillow between knees, or on back with pillow under knees

When to Seek Urgent Care:
- Any red flag symptoms listed above
- Pain lasting > 6 weeks without improvement
- Progressive neurological deficits (weakness, numbness)
- Night pain that wakes from sleep
- Bowel or bladder dysfunction

TITLE: Common Musculoskeletal Injuries - RICE Protocol
SOURCE: American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS), 2023
CATEGORY: Orthopedics | Sports Medicine

The RICE protocol (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation) is the standard first-aid approach for acute soft tissue injuries including sprains, strains, and contusions during the first 48-72 hours.

Updated Protocol (POLICE - Protection, Optimal Loading, Ice, Compression, Elevation):
1. Protection: Avoid activities that increase pain; use brace/splint if needed
2. Optimal Loading: Gentle, pain-free movement to promote healing (complete immobilization may delay recovery)
3. Ice: Apply for 15-20 minutes every 2-3 hours; use barrier (cloth) between ice and skin
4. Compression: Elastic bandage, firm but not tight; loosen if numbness/tingling occurs
5. Elevation: Elevate above heart level when possible to reduce swelling

Sprain vs Strain:
- Sprain: Injury to a ligament (connects bone to bone). Common in ankles, knees, wrists.
- Strain: Injury to a muscle or tendon (connects muscle to bone). Common in back, hamstrings, rotator cuff.

Grades of Sprains:
- Grade 1 (Mild): Microscopic ligament tears, mild swelling, can bear weight
- Grade 2 (Moderate): Partial ligament tear, moderate swelling, difficulty bearing weight
- Grade 3 (Severe): Complete ligament tear, significant swelling, inability to bear weight → Requires medical evaluation

When to Seek Medical Imaging/Care:
- Inability to bear weight immediately after injury or after 4 steps
- Visible deformity of the joint
- Significant bruising or swelling within first hour
- Numbness or loss of pulse below the injury
- No improvement after 72 hours of RICE/POLICE protocol
- Suspected fracture (point tenderness over bone)

TITLE: Headache Classification and Triage
SOURCE: International Headache Society (IHS) Classification, ICHD-3, 2023
CATEGORY: Neurology | General Medicine

Primary Headache Types:

1. Tension-Type Headache (Most Common):
- Bilateral pressing/tightening quality
- Mild to moderate intensity
- Not aggravated by routine physical activity
- No nausea, may have photophobia OR phonophobia (not both)
- Self-care: Hydration, rest, stress management, OTC analgesics

2. Migraine:
- Typically unilateral, pulsating quality
- Moderate to severe intensity
- Aggravated by routine physical activity
- Associated with nausea/vomiting, photophobia AND phonophobia
- May have aura (visual disturbances, sensory symptoms) lasting 5-60 minutes
- Triggers: Stress, sleep changes, hormones, certain foods (aged cheese, alcohol, MSG), weather changes
- Self-care: Dark quiet room, cold compress on forehead, hydration, caffeine in small amounts

3. Cluster Headache:
- Severe, strictly unilateral, orbital/temporal pain
- Duration: 15 minutes to 3 hours, occurring in clusters
- Associated with ipsilateral autonomic features (tearing, nasal congestion, ptosis)
- Requires specialist management

THUNDERCLAP HEADACHE - EMERGENCY:
⚠️ Sudden, severe headache reaching maximum intensity within 60 seconds ("worst headache of my life") → CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES IMMEDIATELY
This may indicate subarachnoid hemorrhage, cerebral venous thrombosis, or other life-threatening conditions. Do NOT wait for symptoms to resolve.

Secondary Headache Red Flags (SNNOOP10 Criteria):
- Systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss)
- Neurological signs (confusion, focal deficits)
- Onset: Sudden/thunderclap
- Older age (new onset after 50)
- Position-dependent (worse lying down or standing)
- Progressive pattern over weeks
- Triggered by Valsalva (coughing, straining)
